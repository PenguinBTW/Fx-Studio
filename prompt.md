# SCENE PROMPT: "THE INTERLOCK" (Child Lock video, Act II → Act IV bridge)

You are building one scene of a YouTube video as a **procedural, code-driven animation**. No imported models, textures, images, fonts or audio. Everything is generated by code. Read the whole brief before writing anything. The visual bar is "film title sequence," not "explainer."

---

## 1. Director's intent

The video starts with a child lock on a car door and slowly reveals it as a physical symbol of one idea: **a system can protect you by making your action stop meaning anything.**

This scene is the turning point. It does three things in order:

1. Shows the one fact that makes the child lock unsettling: **the handle still moves, and nothing happens.** The person's action is not blocked. It is *disconnected*.
2. Strips the mechanism down to a pure diagram of that idea (input, gap, output) and shows the same diagram repeating at every scale of civilisation, until the whole structure is revealed to be one giant lock.
3. Turns it on the viewer: the person at the lever is **you.**

Emotional arc: curiosity, then recognition, then vertigo, then a quiet sting. It must feel inevitable and calm, never frantic. Restraint is what makes it expensive-looking.

**The one rule that carries the meaning: red appears only where a connection is severed (the gap).** Nowhere else, ever. Red must never be used for decoration, UI, warnings or general emphasis. By the end of the scene the audience should read red as "an action that no longer connects to anything."

**Do not use padlock icons anywhere.** The visual language is the lever / gap / latch interlock, not a padlock.

## 4. Timeline

All times in seconds. Each beat flows into the next with no hard cuts.

### BEAT 0: THE SWITCH (0.0 – 8.0)

Black. Light slowly reveals the trailing edge of a car door: a brushed-metal strip, a rubber seal, and the small child-lock slider in its slot (slot ≈ 30 mm long).

- **Geometry (procedural):** extruded rounded-rectangle slot, slider knob with fine grip ridges (generate with a sine ridge displacement on the normals), brushed metal edge using anisotropic noise for the normal map (stretched along one axis), thin rubber seal with slight compression bevel.
- **Camera:** 85 mm-equivalent, shallow depth of field (focus locked on the knob, f/1.4 look), slow dolly forward about 2 cm over the full 8 s with an ease-in-out. Dead steady, no handheld shake (this is the main-channel look).
- **Light:** the strip key fades up from 0 to full over 0.0–3.0 s, sweeping a highlight across the brushed metal from left to right.
- **Action:** at **t = 5.2** the knob slides 14 mm and clicks into place. Motion curve: slow start for 0.6 s, then a fast snap with a critically damped spring and a 2% overshoot. As the knob moves, a **thin sliver of `--signal` red is revealed** in the slot behind it. This is the first red in the film. Marker: `LOCK_CLICK` at 5.2.
- Hold the finished state until 8.0.

### BEAT 1: THE HANDLE THAT STILL WORKS (8.0 – 20.0)

The camera glides forward *through* the slot into an x-ray cross-section of the door interior.

- **Parts (procedural, stylised, not realistic):** an interior door handle lever, a connecting rod, a pivoting pawl, and the latch block. Render as solid `--bone` surfaces with a barycentric wireframe edge glow, with the rest of the door dissolved into faint `--ash` wireframe for context.
- **Camera:** begins side-on, then slowly orbits 25° over the beat while pushing in. DoF narrows around whichever part is moving.
- **Action:** the handle is pulled **three times** (t = 10.0, 12.2, 13.8), each pull a little firmer and quicker than the last. On each pull the rod slides forward along its track, **but the pawl has rotated away**, leaving a visible gap between the rod's end and the latch. The latch never moves.
- **The key visual:** an emissive pulse of light (a bright dash) travels along the rod from the handle toward the latch on every pull. When it reaches the gap it **cannot cross**: it flares in `--signal` red at the rod tip and breaks into 30–60 short sparks that fall away with gravity and fade within 0.8 s. The gap itself glows a thin steady red.
- The message to land silently: *the action happened, the connection did not.*
- Markers: `PULL_1` 10.0, `PULL_2` 12.2, `PULL_3` 13.8.
- 14.5–20.0: the pulls stop. The camera holds, then slowly pushes toward the red gap until it fills the centre of frame. The mechanism sits motionless.

### BEAT 2: ABSTRACTION (20.0 – 30.0)

The mechanism un-builds into a pure diagram.

- **Dissolve:** a world-space dissolve front (a noise-thresholded plane) sweeps from the latch toward the handle over ~6 s. Behind the front, solid surfaces fade to wireframe, then wireframe resolves to flat 2D line art. Leave a thin glowing edge at the front itself.
- **Camera:** perform a dolly-zoom into an **orthographic top-down view**: lerp FOV toward ~1° while dollying back to keep the framing constant. It should arrive flat exactly as the dissolve front completes (t ≈ 27).
- **End state, the Interlock glyph "G" (centred, ~40% of frame width):**
  - left: **input node**, a circle
  - centre: a **gap**, a short break in the line, with a small blocker bar sitting in it and glowing `--signal`
  - right: **output node**, a square
  - a thin line joining input to the gap's left edge and the gap's right edge to the output
- Lines are `--bone`, red only on the blocker and gap. Hold the finished glyph in silence from 27.0 to 30.0. This is the calm before the scale reveal. Marker: `GLYPH_REVEAL` at 27.0.

### BEAT 3: THE SAME LOCK, EVERYWHERE (30.0 – 58.0)

The camera zooms **out** continuously and exponentially. The glyph is revealed to be one cell of something much larger, then larger again, repeating at every scale.

- **Self-similar structure:** the glyph G is built from copies of itself. The input node and the output node are each *another G* at ~1/6 scale. At each zoom level the cell becomes a node of a bigger G. Implement this as a **zoom-fold fragment shader**: render the glyph SDF in a loop over up to 7 octaves, scaling by a fixed ratio per octave, cross-fading adjacent octaves with a smoothstep so there is no visible popping. Fall back to instanced geometry only if the shader approach fails.
- **Zoom curve:** total zoom-out of 10^6 over 28 s using `scale = exp(k · ease(u))`, `u = (t − 30) / 28`, with `ease` a gentle ease-in-out. Maximum zoom speed occurs mid-beat.
- **What the nodes become:** at certain scales the inside of a node resolves into a small **pictogram** drawn purely from SDF shapes, each representing a *different system with the same gap*:
  1. `DOOR`: a door outline with a latch point
  2. `PAYMENT`: a card outline with a cross-out bar
  3. `STEERING`: a wheel with two lane lines pushing it back
  4. `FEED`: stacked content bars with one bar struck out
  5. `BORDER`: a gate with a lowered barrier
  6. `BUTTON`: a greyed button with diagonal hatching

  Each pictogram's own link has the red gap. Label each with its monospace tag (above) at the lower-left of its cell, fading in over 0.3 s and out over 0.3 s as it passes through the "readable" zoom range.
- **Counter:** bottom-right, `--ash` monospace, a running tally of "interlocks in frame" that counts up exponentially: 1, 6, 36, 216 … 1,000,000. Right-aligned, tabular figures, easing smoothly between values (no flicker).
- **Density and colour:** as the zoom goes on, the frame becomes a dense lattice of `--bone` lines with **red pinpoints at every gap**, reading like a field of stars or lit windows in a vast city at night. Red must stay tiny and sparse enough to remain meaningful. Cap red coverage at ~1.5% of pixels.
- **Camera feel:** pure zoom plus an extremely slow rotation (≤ 4° total) so the lattice never feels like a static wallpaper. Add subtle radial motion blur proportional to zoom speed.
- **Climax (≈ 53.0 – 58.0):** the zoom decelerates and the whole dense field finally resolves into **one single, enormous G** filling the frame. The input circle is on the left, the red blocker in the middle, the output square on the right. It must be clear that the entire lattice was *one* interlock. Marker: `ONE_LOCK` at 55.0.
- **At 58.0 everything goes silent and the frame drops to a dim state** (`--ash` lines, red held at 40% brightness).

### BEAT 4: THE INVERSION (58.0 – 72.0)

- **58.0 – 63.0:** slow zoom *in* on the input circle (the left node). It resolves into a **human silhouette built from ~20,000 `--bone` particles** (positions defined analytically by a seeded point-sampled figure, with a gentle idle drift). The figure faces right, one arm extended toward a lever that is the input end of the line. Gentle shimmer, no hard outline.
- **63.0 – 67.0:** the figure reaches. A pulse of light leaves the hand, travels along the line and **dies in the red gap exactly as in Beat 1**: flare, sparks, fade. A small percentage of the figure's particles (≈ 4%) peel off the hand at the moment of impact and drift away, as if some intent leaked out. Marker: `YOU_REACH` at 63.5.
- **67.0 – 70.0:** the camera pulls back as the figure and diagram dissolve back down in scale to the single **slider on the door edge from Beat 0** (a clean reverse of the scale reveal, but 3× faster). The knob now rests **exactly halfway** along the slot, with the red sliver half revealed. It must be visually impossible to tell whether it is on or off. Marker: `HALFWAY` at 70.0.
- **70.0 – 72.0:** hold. Then cut to pure black at 72.0 (no fade; a hard clean cut).

## 5. Procedural systems summary

1. **SDF glyph library:** circle, rounded rect, line, gap with blocker, door, card, wheel, lane lines, content bars, gate, hatched button. All antialiased with `fwidth`-based smoothing.
2. **Zoom-fold shader:** 7 octaves, per-octave fade, the glyph as an IFS-style recursion, with a uniform for red intensity and a uniform for global dim.
3. **Pulse-and-sparks effect:** a single reusable system used in Beat 1 and Beat 4, driven analytically by `(t − t0)`, so the two moments visibly rhyme.
4. **Dissolve front:** one shared world-space noise plane used across Beat 2 for the mechanism-to-diagram transition.
5. **Human particle figure:** 20k points sampled from an analytic skeleton (capsules for limbs, ellipsoid for torso and head) with per-particle seeded drift.

## 6. Post-processing stack (order matters)

1. Tone mapping: ACES filmic, exposure 1.0
2. Bloom: threshold 0.8, strength 0.6, radius 0.5 (picks up only lines and red)
3. Depth of field: Beats 0 and 1 only; focal distance animated to follow the active part
4. Radial motion blur: Beat 3 only, 8 samples, strength tied to zoom speed
5. Chromatic aberration: radial, 0.0006 base, rising to 0.0014 at peak zoom speed in Beat 3
6. Vignette: 0.25
7. Film grain: luma only, 0.035 intensity, updated at 24 Hz

No lens flares, no light leaks, no glitch effects.

## 8. Things to avoid

- Fast cuts, shake, glitching, or anything that makes the scene feel panicked.
- Extra colours, decorative gradients, or lens effects beyond the stack above.
- Any text beyond the six monospace labels and the counter.
- Making the figure in Beat 4 cartoonish or detailed. It should be abstract, tender and slightly ghostlike.
- Any literal explanation on screen. The scene works by showing, not telling.
