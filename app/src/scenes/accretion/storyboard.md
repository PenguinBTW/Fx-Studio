# accretion — storyboard

A proto-planetary disk around a young star: thousands of dust grains in Keplerian shear — inner orbits fast, outer slow — streaming in bands around a white-hot core. Slowly the camera drifts as a brighter clump of matter coalesces mid-disk and shepherds a gap in the ring. 30s, single continuous shot.

## Beats
- 0–6s: the disk established — bands of dust streaming, differential rotation visible, core blazing.
- 6–15s: shear becomes legible — inner grains visibly lap outer ones; occasional glints as grains catch the light.
- 15–24s: the clump — a brighter knot coalesces at mid-radius, drawing material; a shallow gap darkens around its orbit.
- 24–30s: the new body settles into its lane, the gap held open, the disk streaming on.

## Camera specification
- Lens: normal-wide; aperture engine-scale 5 for deep-space depth layering.
- Position vector: slow orbital drift, radius ~5.5, pitch descending 0.55→0.4, yaw advancing ~0.5 rad — the disk's inclination reads as a sculptural ellipse.
- Focal point: the clump once it forms; the disk mid-band before.
- Movement language: patient drift with minimal handheld — this is a telescope, not a documentary.
