# rainfall — visual plan

## Register
Hydrology — rain as a water-surface phenomenon. The catalog has atmosphere (cloudfall), diffusion (inkwell), fluids (mercury) but nothing about *rain itself*: impact, rings, interference. The subject is the puddle as an instrument — every drop a note, the surface a writing medium that records the storm in overlapping rings.

## Metaphor
A dark mirror being continuously rewritten. Rain doesn't fall — it *arrives*: each impact is a small punctuation, rings expand, interfere, die. The streetlight out of frame turns every ring into a brief bright arc.

## Materials
- Surface: wet asphalt-stone, near-black, mirror-smooth where water sheets, rougher at the edges
- Rain: silver-backlit streaks, wind-slanted
- Light: single amber sodium streetlight out of frame, upper-left — everything warm-lit against blue-black night

## Anti-cliché audit
- NOT romantic bokeh-city rain: no neon signs, no umbrellas, no city — pure water physics close-up
- NOT uniform drop sizes: ring scale varies with drop momentum; drizzle vs downpour have different ring textures
- NOT static intensity: the storm breathes — build, peak, ease

## Focal hierarchy
1. Ring interference patterns in the lit pool of the surface
2. Rain streak texture against the dark sky portion
3. The horizon-less dark edge where surface meets night

## Changes over time
| Time | Beat | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8s | drizzle | sparse rings, isolated, fully visible lifetimes; rain streaks thin |
| 8–20s | downpour | ring density saturates — interference dominates; streaks dense, surface never settles |
| 20–30s | easing | density thins; individual rings become readable again; final seconds near-stillness with lingering large rings |
