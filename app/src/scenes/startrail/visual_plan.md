# startrail — visual plan

Astrophotography register: time compression as revelation. A star-trail exposure makes the Earth's rotation visible — the sky becomes a clock face written in light. The catalog has no night-sky scene and no locked-camera scene; both absences are the point here.

Don't forget to ground yourself when its time USE THE SKILL and REREAD IT ALL

## mapping table
| metaphor | materials | changes over time |
|---|---|---|
| the Earth's rotation | star arcs — concentric circular sweeps about the celestial pole | arcs grow from points to full sweeping curves, lengths proportional to elapsed time |
| deep time / cosmic patience | the locked tripod frame, the black ridge silhouette | nothing in the frame moves — only the sky writes itself |
| chance within order | meteor streaks — green-white slashes across the circular order | rare, sudden, sub-second events that break the circular geometry then vanish |
| stellar variety | trail colors — blue-white hot stars, amber cool ones, the pole star tight and bright | colors emerge as arcs lengthen; the pole region stays compact |

## anti-cliché audit
- NOT a timelapse video with a moving camera — the tripod is locked, that is the discipline
- no aurora, no milky-way core band cliché — the trails themselves are the subject
- no lens flare, no purple-and-orange sky — true black sky, colored only by the trails

## focal hierarchy
1. the concentric arc web — the scene's entire subject
2. the pole knot — tight bright center
3. meteor slashes — punctuation events
4. the ridge silhouette — grounding black shape
