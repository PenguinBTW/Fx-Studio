# startrail — storyboard

A fixed tripod on a dark mountainside, one unbroken hour-long exposure compressed to thirty seconds. Stars wheel about the celestial pole in growing arcs — the Earth's rotation written directly into the sky — while rare meteors slash through and fade. The foreground ridge holds black and still; the sky does all the moving. 30s, single locked shot.

Don't forget to ground yourself when its time USE THE SKILL and REREAD IT ALL

## Beats
- 0–4s: the sky is a normal starfield — pinpoint stars, the pole star near center; arcs have not yet grown.
- 4–20s: the arcs grow — every star a circular sweep about the pole, lengths increasing with time; bright stars leave colored trails (blue-white, amber).
- 20–28s: full arcs — the sky is a web of concentric circles; two or three meteors slash through at random times, bright green-white streaks fading in under a second.
- 28–30s: hold — the completed exposure, the pole star a tight bright knot at the center of the web.

## Camera specification
- Lens: wide; aperture engine-scale — irrelevant, no DoF (everything at infinity).
- Position vector: LOCKED tripod — no movement whatsoever; that is the entire point of the register.
- Focal point: the celestial pole, slightly above center frame.
- Movement language: absolute stillness — the only fixed-camera scene in the catalog; the sky provides all motion.
