# lull: agent workflow feedback (post-mortem)

Feedback from the AI agent that made *lull* (a 3:11 first-person horror film,
[fxstudio-demos](https://github.com/PenguinBTW/fxstudio-demos/tree/main/scenes/lull)) end to end in f(x) Studio, over about 90
iterations of stills, contact sheets, clips and full renders.

## What worked well
- **$f(t)$ determinism and one clock.** A single pure-TypeScript timeline drove the camera path, torch, story cues and the sound
  script, so picture and audio never drifted. A late retime (tightening 3:36 to 3:11) was a story-time to film-time mapping, not
  a rewrite. Re-renders were pixel-identical.
- **The headless CLI.** `stills`, `sheet`, short `video` clips, `--4k` and audio muxing covered almost every check. Contact
  sheets were the agent's main way of seeing the film.
- **Blender in the loop.** `build.py` → `model.glb` with `fx_part` named parts was smooth, including baking a real cloth
  simulation into an asset. `assets_check` caught real mistakes.
- **Full Three.js access.** Custom water caustics, a volumetric torch beam and shader injections were all possible; there was
  no creative ceiling.
- **Post overrides.** Per-scene `shake`, `zoom`, `ca` and `fade` made jump-scare hits and blackouts one-liners.
- **Skills as guardrails.** The grounding and anti-slop rules caught weak work; a couple of rules (diegetic-only text) needed
  explicit user overrides.

## Where the agent was slowed down
1. **Render speed for iteration.** About 10–13 fps at 1080p means about 20 minutes per full film, and each `stills` call boots
   Vite and Chrome (30–60 s). Motion problems were judged from stills, so some slipped through.
2. **One render at a time.** A second render's Vite fails to start while a video render is running (a `port busy` error), which
   also blocked a parallel reviewer agent. The error doesn't say why.
3. **Exposure adaptation.** The automatic sensor exposure makes very dark scenes hard to predict, and it also scales
   camera-attached overlay cards (title cards needed `toneMapped: false` and care).
4. **Posing imported parts.** Blender-side part rotations are not obvious from code, and there is no built-in asset inspector;
   a turntable project had to be written by hand, and aiming one arm took six renders.
5. **No audio feedback for agents.** Agents cannot listen; loudness and spectrum measurements plus the user's ears were the only
   checks. The film's sound was generated by a custom script rather than `defineMix`, because synthesised cue lists in code were
   simpler than stems.
6. **Project-specific checks.** `test:seams` and the determinism scan cover a fixed list of projects; new projects have to add
   themselves.

## Requests, in priority order
1. A **draft render mode** (lower resolution and quality, e.g. `--draft`) for fast motion checks.
2. A **persistent render server** (or a clear "render in progress" message) so renders can run in parallel without the start-up cost.
3. A **low-res proxy with audio** for watching a whole cut quickly.
4. A built-in **asset inspector / turntable** that prints part names, pivots and default rotations.
5. **Audio analysis for agents**: per-cue loudness, peak and spectral summaries around given timestamps, and a way to declare
   synthesised cue lists in `defineMix`.
6. Determinism and seam checks that **discover projects automatically**.
